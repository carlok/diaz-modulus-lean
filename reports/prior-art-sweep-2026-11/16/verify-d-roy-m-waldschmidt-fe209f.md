**Explanation.** Th. 0.2 is printed at p. 755, proof at p. 791. Neither target is printed; (Qr2) is not mentioned. Both follow as below, by my own argument.

Pair: take (u,ū,v,v̄) in L^4, P=X1X2−cX3X4 with c=|u|²/|v|² in Q. Th. 0.2 gives a rational subspace U containing the point, with U in V. Restricted to U, the coordinates are rational linear forms, all nonzero since the point's coordinates are nonzero. P vanishes on U, so X1X2=cX3X4 in the UFD Q[U]. Hence X3 is a rational multiple of X1 or X2. Evaluating at the point gives v in Qu or Qū.

(Qr2): take (ℓ0,ℓ̄0,ℓ1,ℓ̄1), P=X0Y1∓X1Y0, the sign from ℓ1/ℓ0 being real or imaginary. All coordinates are nonzero, as 0 is real. Factorisation gives X0 proportional to X1 or to Y0. If ℓ0=qℓ̄0 with q rational, then |q|=1, so ℓ0 is real or imaginary, excluded. So ℓ1/ℓ0 is in Q.
