# Entry 16, strategy: own-field

## Target, as restated by the searcher

Two statements about logarithms of algebraic numbers (L = {z : e^z algebraic, nonzero}), each assuming the field generated by the numbers and their complex conjugates has transcendence degree at most 1 over Q. (A) Qr2 in trdeg one: if l0, l1 in L are neither real nor purely imaginary and l1/l0 is real or purely imaginary, then l1/l0 is rational. (B) Pair rigidity: if u, v in L are nonzero and |u|^2/|v|^2 is rational, then v is in Qu or in Q u-bar. Both say a 2x2 matrix of logarithms built from the numbers and their conjugates has rank one only for trivial reasons: (l0, l1; l0-bar, l1-bar) for A and (u, v; c v-bar, u-bar) with c = |u|^2/|v|^2 for B. The search is for a printed source stating A or B, or implying them other than through the named Brownawell Corollaries 5 and 7 and the four exponentials theorem in trdeg one.

## Leads

### Lead 1: implies

- **Citation:** D. Roy, M. Waldschmidt, Approximation diophantienne et indépendance algébrique de logarithmes, Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 30 (1997), 753-796
- **Location:** Théorème 0.2, p. 755; proved in §7, pp. 790-791. Statement checked on the page image: P has coefficients in Q and the subspace is defined over Q
- **Quote:** ou bien le corps Q(λ1,...,λn) est de degré de transcendance ≥ 2 sur Q
- **Link:** https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1997_4_30_6_753_0
- **Relation:** My own translation; the paper does not state A or B. Th. 0.2: a point of L^n on a rational homogeneous quadric, in trdeg ≤ 1, lies in a Q-subspace U contained in the quadric. (B) Apply it to P = X1X2 - cX3X4 at (u, u-bar, v, v-bar). P has rank 4, so dim U ≤ 2. If dim U = 1, then v is in Qu. If u, u-bar are Q-independent and dim U = 2, write v = au + b u-bar, v-bar = a u-bar + bu with a, b rational. P vanishing on the plane forces ab = 0, so v is in Qu or Q u-bar. If u-bar = ±u and dim U = 2, P restricted to the plane has a nonzero x^2 term, which is impossible. (A) Apply it to P = X1X4 - εX2X3 at (l0, l0-bar, l1, l1-bar), where the conjugate of l1/l0 is ε times l1/l0. Here l0, l0-bar are Q-independent, so l1 = a l0 + b l0-bar, and vanishing on the plane forces b = 0, with a = 0 if ε = -1. So l1/l0 is rational. This is a generalization of the input already named (Th. 0.2 contains Brownawell's Cor. 7 as the determinant case) and is no deeper than the named route. It is not a printed statement of the target.

### Lead 2: implies

- **Citation:** D. Roy, M. Waldschmidt, Quadratic relations between logarithms of algebraic numbers, Proc. Japan Acad. Ser. A 71 (1995), 151-153
- **Location:** Theorem 2, p. 151 (announcement; the proof is deferred to the 1997 paper). Theorem 1 there is the trdeg-one four exponentials theorem, citing Brownawell Cor. 7
- **Quote:** any homogeneous polynomial of degree 2
- **Link:** https://projecteuclid.org/download/pdf_1/euclid.pja/1195510604
- **Relation:** Same statement as Th. 0.2 of the 1997 paper, so the same derivation applies. The OCR of this page is scrambled across columns; I relied on the 1997 version for the exact wording. No conjugates appear in this note.

### Lead 3: analogue only

- **Citation:** D. Roy, M. Waldschmidt, Ann. Sci. ENS 30 (1997), same paper as the first lead
- **Location:** Corollaires 7.3 and 7.4, pp. 791-792
- **Quote:** No separate quote: one quote per source, already used for Th. 0.2. Paraphrase in derivation.
- **Link:** https://www.numdam.org/item/ASENS_1997_4_30_6_753_0
- **Relation:** The closest printed statements with both conjugation and trdeg one. Cor. 7.3: for a logarithm λ neither real nor purely imaginary and algebraically dependent with its conjugate, and q a rational binary form of nonzero discriminant, exp of the square root of q(λ, λ-bar) is transcendental. Cor. 7.4: for non-real λ in L, either λ and λ-bar are algebraically independent or e^|λ| is transcendental. These concern one logarithm and whether a modulus-type quantity is itself a logarithm. Neither gives A or B, which compare two logarithms. They do show the authors applied the method to (λ, λ-bar), but they did not state the two-logarithm case.

## Notes

**Result.** I found no printed statement of (A) or (B). The authors' "not found" stands as a claim about explicit statements. There is one substantive lead that goes beyond the named sources: Roy-Waldschmidt 1997, Théorème 0.2 (announced in 1995 as Theorem 2). It implies both statements, but only through a short translation of my own.

**How strong the lead is.**
- Th. 0.2 was not on the target's nearby list.
- It is stronger than Brownawell's Cor. 7, since any rational quadric replaces the 2x2 determinant.
- It adds no depth for this target. The target's own route (Cor. 7 at (l1/l0, l0-bar/l0, l0) and (v/u, c v-bar/u, u)) is simpler.
- A verifier should treat it as a second, more general source for the same routine consequence. It is not a prior statement of the result, and it does not remove the "routine" label.
- With the 1997 paper, a fair citation for the entry would be "immediate from [Bro74, Cor. 7]; also a case of [RW97, Th. 0.2]".

**My checks on the derivation.**
- I did the linear algebra by hand: maximal isotropic subspaces of a rank-4 quadric have dimension 2, followed by a coefficient comparison on the plane. I did not machine-check it.
- For (A), the purely imaginary case with l1 nonzero is excluded outright.
- For (B), P has rational coefficients because c is rational.
- The page image of p. 755 confirms P in Q[X] and U defined over Q. Conjecture 0.3, just below it, uses Q-bar.

**What the sources say relative to the target.**
- Diaz 2007 states (Qr2) on p. 376 and derives it from the four exponentials conjecture on p. 377. On p. 386 he quotes the Brownawell-Waldschmidt trdeg-one theorem, in exactly the form needed, in a remark. He illustrates it only with l^2, l^3. A few lines later he restates (Qr2) and proves only Cor. 6, which assumes the ratio is in L-tilde. He does not draw the trdeg-one consequence. The gap is real and is one line wide.
- Diaz 2004 Th. 1-3 use conjugation with the six exponentials theorem and its strong form. There is no trdeg-one hypothesis.
- Waldschmidt's role-of-conjugation paper surveys Diaz's route through the strong six exponentials theorem. I saw no trdeg-one use in the parts I read and in its keyword hits.
- RW97 Cor. 7.3 and 7.4 are the nearest in spirit, with λ and its conjugate in trdeg one, but they concern one logarithm.
- DALAG Cor. 15.28(c) is the same four exponentials input. A search for complex conjugation there found only incidental hits.

**What I could not settle.**
- Forward citations of Diaz 2004, Diaz 2007 and RW97 are incomplete, because the OpenAlex and arXiv APIs returned 429 and Semantic Scholar is thin. A recent paper could exist that these searches missed, though zbMATH and web searches surfaced none.
- Books such as Fel'dman-Nesterenko and Murty-Rath were not opened. An exercise there is possible.
- Many local files were screened by keyword only, and OCR can drop bars, so a statement written purely in bar notation with no word for conjugation could be missed.

**Method note.** While fetching Waldschmidt's PDFs I wrote a Python call with TLS certificate verification turned off, after verification had failed earlier. The permission system blocked it and nothing was fetched. I did not repeat that. I then used JavaScript fetch with normal verification for zbMATH, and the sanctioned fetch tool for two pages. Those Waldschmidt notes stay unread, as listed under inaccessible.
