## Notation

- Q̄ (`\Qbar`) is the field of algebraic numbers. ℒ is the set of logarithms of non-zero algebraic numbers; ℒ̃ = Q̄ + Q̄ℒ.
- A *point of a circle* is u ∈ ℂ, u ≠ 0, with uū algebraic. Then ū = ρ/u with ρ = uū, so conjugation acts on rational functions of u.
- H₀ = Q̄ + Q̄u + Q̄ū.
- A *p × q configuration over K* in a K-space V ⊆ ℂ is x₁,…,x_p and y₁,…,y_q, each family K-linearly independent, with every product x_i y_j in V. So a 2 × 3 configuration says V contains a product of a 2-dimensional and a 3-dimensional subspace, in the sense of all pairwise products lying in V.
- A *candidate* is u ≠ 0 with |u| and e^u algebraic; Diaz's conjecture says there is none. The *conjugation hull* of z is W_z = H₀ + Q̄z + Q̄z̄.
- A space is *invisible to four-dimensional extensions* when it carries a 2 × 3 configuration but no H₀ + Q̄w inside it carries one.

### DiazModulus.conj_equivariant_degree_one_space_eq_real_pencil_space

Let $u\in\C\setminus\Qbar$ and $s\in\Qbar$ real with $s^{2}=u\bar u$. If $1,u,\bar u,g,ug$ are free and $\bar g\in\Qbar+\Qbar u+\Qbar ug$, then $\Qbar+\Qbar u+\Qbar\bar u+\Qbar g+\Qbar ug$ is a space of Proposition~\ref{prop:conj_stable_real_pencil_space_carries_two_by_three_configuration}.

### DiazModulus.real_pencil_quartic_configuration_invisible_to_four_dimensional_extensions

Let $u\in\C\setminus\Qbar$, let $s\in\Qbar$ be real with $s^{2}=u\bar u$, $f=u^{2}/s^{2}+s^{2}/u^{2}$, and let $W$ be the span of $1,u,\bar u,f,f/(s+u)$. Then $W$ is stable under conjugation and carries a $2\times3$ configuration, and no $\Qbar+\Qbar u+\Qbar\bar u+\Qbar w$ with $w\in W$ outside $\Qbar+\Qbar u+\Qbar\bar u$ does.

Source line: Not found in the sources read; routine (Chapter~\ref{chap:14-not-found-in-the-sources}). Its polar divisor has degree $5$, so $W$ contains no progression: the conjugation-stable analogue of Proposition~\ref{prop:laurent_case_two_configuration_invisible}. It is the case $k=2$, $\lambda=1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:laurent_real_pencil_configuration_invisible_to_four_dimensional_extensions}.
