- **Source read:** B. Reznick, On the length of binary forms, arXiv:1007.5485v1 (30 Jul 2010), full PDF text read from https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.5485. Theorem 2.1 on p. 4, Corollary 2.2 on p. 7. The Springer 2013 version was not opened.

- **Passage:** "the minimal degree of a Sylvester form for f which splits completely over K"

**Hypotheses.** Nothing matches. Reznick: binary forms of degree d over a field K in C; the question is the length of powers of linear forms. Target: 5-dimensional Q̄-subspaces of C, generated by rational functions of u with uū algebraic. No configuration, no product of subspaces, no genericity condition. Complex conjugation of u does not occur. Reznick's only 'conjugation' (Theorem 4.6) is the Galois conjugation of a quadratic extension K(√u) acting on summands of a form, a different object.

**Explanation.** The citation is accurate as a citation: Sylvester's theorem (Theorem 2.1, p. 4) says f is a combination of d-th powers of the linear factors of a squarefree h exactly when a Hankel system (2.4) holds. Corollary 2.2 (p. 7, not p. 4) gives K-length as the minimal degree of a Sylvester form splitting over K.

Neither concerns the target. A text search for pencil, configuration, invisible and modulus found nothing relevant; 'circle' occurs only for quadrature formulas. The target has two parts: every conjugation-stable space with 1,u,ū,g,ug free and ḡ in Q̄+Q̄u+Q̄ug is a real pencil space; and the explicit span of 1,u,ū,f,f/(s+u) carries a 2×3 configuration that no four-dimensional H₀+Q̄w carries. Neither appears.

I could supply no derivation. Hankel kernels describe decompositions of a form's coefficients, not bilinear products of subspaces of a function field. At most, Hankel shape resembles a progression, and the target's example has none. Only a loose analogy (rank conditions, linear recurrences), not even a usable one. Verdict: no prior art.
