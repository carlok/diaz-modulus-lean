# Entry 15: Theorem D and its barrier

Chapter 14 class: routine.

## The part not found, on its own

At a point u of a circle transcendental over the field of ℒ̃, the space ℒ_u = ℒ + ℚu + ℚū satisfies, relative to ℒ̃, the formal properties of Baker, Hermite–Lindemann, Gel'fond–Schneider, six and four exponentials and Roy, so those theorems, used as properties of (ℒ, ℒ̃) alone, cannot prove Diaz's conjecture. Look for any such model or barrier: a set closed under these statements containing an element with algebraic modulus, or a metatheorem of this type.

## Printed results named as nearby (these do not count as finds)

Each clause restates a classical theorem with ℒ replaced by ℒ_u; the classical theorems do not count.

## Notation

- Q̄ (`\Qbar`) is the field of algebraic numbers. ℒ is the set of logarithms of non-zero algebraic numbers; ℒ̃ = Q̄ + Q̄ℒ.
- A *point of a circle* is u ∈ ℂ, u ≠ 0, with uū algebraic. Then ū = ρ/u with ρ = uū, so conjugation acts on rational functions of u.
- H₀ = Q̄ + Q̄u + Q̄ū.
- A *p × q configuration over K* in a K-space V ⊆ ℂ is x₁,…,x_p and y₁,…,y_q, each family K-linearly independent, with every product x_i y_j in V. So a 2 × 3 configuration says V contains a product of a 2-dimensional and a 3-dimensional subspace, in the sense of all pairwise products lying in V.
- A *candidate* is u ≠ 0 with |u| and e^u algebraic; Diaz's conjecture says there is none. The *conjugation hull* of z is W_z = H₀ + Q̄z + Q̄z̄.
- A space is *invisible to four-dimensional extensions* when it carries a 2 × 3 configuration but no H₀ + Q̄w inside it carries one.

## The entry, as in chapter 14

Theorem~\ref{thm:logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_theorem_D} (Theorem~D), with Proposition~\ref{prop:span_logAlg_theorem_D_properties} and Corollary~\ref{cor:theorem_D_barrier}: at a point $u$ of a circle that is transcendental over the field of $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$, the space $\mathcal L_u=\mathcal L+\Q u+\Q\bar u$ has the properties of Baker, Hermite–Lindemann, Gel'fond–Schneider, six and four exponentials and, relative to $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$, Roy, as $\mathcal L$ has; it contains $u$, which is non-zero with $|u|$ algebraic, so these theorems, used as properties of $(\mathcal L,\widetilde{\mathcal L})$, cannot prove Diaz's conjecture. Each clause restates a classical theorem with $\mathcal L$ replaced by $\mathcal L_u$; the six exponentials clause is proved without Roy's theorem (Proposition~\ref{prop:logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_six_exponentials}), and the Roy clause is Theorem~\ref{thm:logAlgTilde_adjoin_circle_point_no_two_by_three_configuration}, listed above. The barrier concerns membership in $\mathcal L_u$ only, not the values of the exponential function.

## Statements, copied from the blueprint

### DiazModulus.logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_theorem_D

Let $\mathcal L$ be the set of logarithms of algebraic numbers, the $z\in\C$ with $e^{z}$ algebraic, and $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$ the $\Qbar$-span of $1$ and $\mathcal L$. Let $F$ be the field generated by $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$, let $u\in\C$ be transcendental over $F$ with $u\bar u$ algebraic, and put $\mathcal L_u=\mathcal L+\Q u+\Q\bar u$. Say that a $\Q$-vector subspace $L\subseteq\C$ has the \emph{Theorem~D properties} if, with $\widetilde L$ the $\Qbar$-span of $1$ and $L$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{conjugation:} $\bar z\in L$ for every $z\in L$;
\item \emph{Baker:} if $l_1,\dots,l_n\in L$ are linearly independent over $\Q$ and $b_0,b_1,\dots,b_n$ are algebraic, not all zero, then $b_0+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_il_i\neq0$;
\item \emph{Hermite–Lindemann:} $0$ is the only algebraic element of $L$;
\item \emph{Gel'fond–Schneider:} if $l_0\neq0$ and $\beta l_0$ lie in $L$ and $\beta$ is algebraic, then $\beta\in\Q$;
\item \emph{Roy, relative to $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$:} if there is no $2\times3$ configuration in $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$, there is none in $\widetilde L$;
\item \emph{six exponentials:} there are no $x_1,x_2$, linearly independent over $\Q$, and $y_1,y_2,y_3$, linearly independent over $\Q$, with all six products $x_iy_j$ in $L$;
\item \emph{four exponentials in transcendence degree one:} if $\ell_{11},\ell_{12},\ell_{21},\ell_{22}\in L$ are non-zero, $\ell_{11}\ell_{22}=\ell_{12}\ell_{21}$ and $\trdeg_{\Q}\Q[\ell_{11},\ell_{12},\ell_{21},\ell_{22}]\leq1$, then the rows, or the columns, of $(\ell_{ij})$ are linearly dependent over $\Q$.
\end{itemize}
Then $\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L_u$, $u\in\mathcal L_u$, $u\notin\Qbar$, $u\neq0$ and $|u|$ is algebraic, and $\mathcal L_u$ has the Theorem~D properties; for it, $\widetilde L=\widetilde{\mathcal L}+\Qbar u+\Qbar\bar u$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mem_logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_iff}). The Roy clause is an implication, so the statement is unconditional, and $e^{u}$ is not involved.

Source line: Not found in the sources read; routine (Chapter~\ref{chap:14-not-found-in-the-sources}). With $\mathcal L$ replaced by $\mathcal L_u$, each clause restates a classical theorem: Baker \cite{Bak66}, Hermite–Lindemann \cite{Her1873,Lin1882}, Gel'fond–Schneider \cite{Gel34,Sch34}, six exponentials \cite[Ch.~II]{Lang66}, \cite{Ram68}, four exponentials in transcendence degree one \cite[Cor.~4]{W73}, \cite[Cor.~7]{Bro74}, and, as an implication, Roy's strong six exponentials theorem \cite[Corollary 2]{Roy92}.

### DiazModulus.span_logAlg_theorem_D_properties

Let $\mathcal L$ be the set of logarithms of algebraic numbers, the $z\in\C$ with $e^{z}$ algebraic, and $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$ the $\Qbar$-span of $1$ and $\mathcal L$. Let $L=\operatorname{span}_{\Q}\mathcal L$, which is $\mathcal L$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:mem_logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_iff}). Then Diaz's conjecture, that $e^{u}$ is transcendental for every $u\neq0$ with $|u|$ algebraic, holds if and only if no non-zero $u\in L$ has $|u|$ algebraic; and $L$ has the Theorem~D properties of Theorem~\ref{thm:logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_theorem_D}, with $\widetilde L=\widetilde{\mathcal L}$.

Source line: Not found in the sources read as a bundle; routine (Chapter~\ref{chap:14-not-found-in-the-sources}). The clauses are classical theorems about $\mathcal L$: Baker \cite{Bak66}, Hermite–Lindemann \cite{Her1873,Lin1882}, Gel'fond–Schneider \cite{Gel34,Sch34}, six exponentials \cite[Ch.~II]{Lang66}, \cite{Ram68} and four exponentials in transcendence degree one \cite[Cor.~4]{W73}, \cite[Cor.~7]{Bro74}; the Roy clause is relative, so Roy's theorem \cite[Corollary 2]{Roy92} is not needed.

### DiazModulus.theorem_D_barrier

Let $\mathcal L$ be the set of logarithms of algebraic numbers, the $z\in\C$ with $e^{z}$ algebraic, and $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$ the $\Qbar$-span of $1$ and $\mathcal L$. There is a $\Q$-vector subspace $L\subseteq\C$ with $\mathcal L\subseteq L$ that has the Theorem~D properties of Theorem~\ref{thm:logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_theorem_D} and contains some $u\neq0$ with $|u|$ algebraic. So these properties, as properties of $(\mathcal L,\widetilde{\mathcal L})$, with Roy's theorem for $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$ \cite[Corollary 2]{Roy92} supplying the hypothesis of the Roy clause, do not prove Diaz's conjecture: by Proposition~\ref{prop:span_logAlg_theorem_D_properties} the conjecture says that no such $u$ lies in $\mathcal L$, and an argument from the properties alone would apply to $L$ as well. The barrier concerns membership only: $L$ comes with no exponential function, so it says nothing about arguments through the values $e^{x_iy_j}$, such as \cite{W73}, or through Roy's rank bounds for larger matrices.

Source line: Not found in the sources read; routine (Chapter~\ref{chap:14-not-found-in-the-sources}), given Theorem~\ref{thm:logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_theorem_D} and Lemma~\ref{lem:exists_circle_point_transcendental_over_logAlgTilde_closure}.

### DiazModulus.logAlg_adjoin_circle_point_six_exponentials

Let $\mathcal L$ be the set of logarithms of algebraic numbers, the $z\in\C$ with $e^{z}$ algebraic, and $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$ the $\Qbar$-span of $1$ and $\mathcal L$. Let $F$ be the field generated by $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$, let $u\in\C$ be transcendental over $F$ with $u\bar u$ algebraic, and put $\mathcal L_u=\mathcal L+\Q u+\Q\bar u$. Then there are no $x_1,x_2$, linearly independent over $\Q$, and $y_1,y_2,y_3$, linearly independent over $\Q$, with all six products $x_iy_j$ in $\mathcal L_u$.

Source line: Restates the six exponentials theorem \cite[Ch.~II]{Lang66}, \cite{Ram68}, \cite[Theorem 1.12]{Wal00} (Theorem~\ref{thm:six_exponentials}), with $\mathcal L$ replaced by $\mathcal L_u$; the dependent cases use Baker's theorem \cite{Bak66}. The $\mathcal L_u$ form, and a route to it that avoids Roy's strong six exponentials theorem \cite{Roy92}, were not found in the sources read; routine (Chapter~\ref{chap:14-not-found-in-the-sources}).

### DiazModulus.logAlgTilde_adjoin_circle_point_no_two_by_three_configuration

Assume Roy's theorem: there is no $2\times3$ configuration in $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$. If $u\bar u\in\Qbar$ and $u$ is transcendental over the field generated by $\widetilde{\mathcal L}$, there is none in $\widetilde{\mathcal L}+\Qbar u+\Qbar\bar u$ either.
