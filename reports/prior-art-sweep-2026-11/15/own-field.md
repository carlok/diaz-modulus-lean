# Entry 15, strategy: own-field

## Target, as restated by the searcher

Let L be the set of logarithms of non-zero algebraic numbers and L~ = Qbar + Qbar L. Let u be non-zero, with u*conj(u) algebraic, and transcendental over the field generated by L~. Then L_u = L + Qu + Q conj(u) is conjugation-stable and satisfies, as bare membership statements: Baker (Q-independent elements are independent over Qbar together with 1), Hermite-Lindemann, Gel'fond-Schneider, six exponentials, four exponentials in transcendence degree one, and "no 2x3 configuration in L~ implies none in L~ + Qbar u + Qbar conj(u)". Since L_u contains L and also contains u, which is non-zero with algebraic modulus, those theorems used only as properties of (L, L~) cannot prove Diaz's conjecture. The search is for any printed model or barrier of this kind: a set closed under such statements that contains an element with algebraic modulus, or a metatheorem saying known transcendence statements formally cannot reach such a conclusion.

## Leads

### Lead 1: analogue only

- **Citation:** M. Waldschmidt, Diophantine Approximation on Linear Algebraic Groups, Grundlehren 326, Springer, 2000. Reports Roy 1989, Propositions 1 and 2, and Roy 1995, Theorem 3.4.
- **Location:** Section 12.1.6, pp. 430-431 (diagram, Lemmas 12.15 and 12.16, motivating example); Section 12.4.1, p. 437; Exercises 12.9 and 12.10
- **Quote:** it is not possible to go from one row to the row above it
- **Link:** https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~michel.waldschmidt/articles/pdf/dalag.pdf
- **Relation:** A printed metatheorem of the same type for abstract triples (k,K,E), but neither L_u nor Diaz's conjecture appears. Lemma 12.15: for x transcendental over k, some infinite-dimensional E in k[x] has Property (A B; C 0), so that property (which would give four exponentials) could not give two algebraically independent logarithms. Lemma 12.16 and Exercise 12.10: any E of dimension at most 3 has rank at least (d+l)/4 for matrices with independent rows and columns. MY TRANSLATION, not printed: with k=Qbar, c^2=u*conj(u) and x=c/u, the printed example E=<u,ux,ux^2> is exactly H0=Qbar+Qbar u+Qbar conj(u), and [[u,c],[c,conj(u)]] has rank 1 with rstr 2. So the rank-half bound cannot exclude H0, and d+l=5 gives no 2x3 configuration in H0. Also mine: k=Q, x=u, p1=u in Exercise 12.9 gives E in Q[u] containing u and satisfying Baker, Hermite-Lindemann, Gel'fond-Schneider, six and four exponentials. But E does not contain L and is not conjugation-stable, so this does not reach the target's L_u.

### Lead 2: analogue only

- **Citation:** D. Roy, Points whose coordinates are logarithms of algebraic numbers on algebraic varieties, Acta Math. 175 (1995), 49-73.
- **Location:** Introduction p. 50; Section 3.2 'Homogeneous surfaces' and Theorem 3.4, pp. 65-66
- **Quote:** it cannot lead to a proof of the four exponentials conjecture
- **Link:** https://projecteuclid.org/download/pdf_1/euclid.acta/1485890866
- **Relation:** Roy's barrier: points of xy=z^2 with independent coordinates cannot be excluded, because every linear embedding gives matrices that 'formally satisfy' Waldschmidt's rank theorem (Theorem 1.2), by Theorem 3.4 on 3-dimensional spans. The target differs: the conclusion is four exponentials, not Diaz's conjecture, the formal statement is only the rank theorem, and the model is a 3-dimensional span that does not contain L. MY TRANSLATION: (lambda, conj(lambda), |lambda|) lies on xy=z^2, so the surface carries Diaz's modulus conjecture (M) for lambda in L, but Roy does not say this and it is not the algebraic-modulus form.

### Lead 3: analogue only

- **Citation:** S. Fischler, Orbits under algebraic groups and logarithms of algebraic numbers, Acta Arith. 100 (2001), 167-187.
- **Location:** Closing N.B. of Section 6, p. 186; cites Roy 1989, Proposition 2
- **Quote:** it is impossible ([8], Proposition 2) to derive "algebraically" any of these conjectures
- **Link:** local library: (local library) others/fischler-2001-acta-arith-orbits-logarithms.txt (no web URL checked)
- **Relation:** A printed statement of the barrier kind: Fischler's conjectures for V(2,2) and T(2,2), consequences of four exponentials, cannot be derived algebraically from the rank theorem (his Theorem 2.1). It concerns neither moduli, nor conjugation, nor a space containing L.

### Lead 4: analogue only

- **Citation:** M. Bays and J. Kirby, Pseudo-exponential maps, variants, and quasiminimality, arXiv:1512.04262v3 (journal version not opened).
- **Location:** Section 9.2 'Incorporating a counterexample to Schanuel's conjecture'
- **Quote:** soft methods which ignore transcendental number theory and analytic considerations cannot hope to work
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.04262
- **Relation:** Builds exponential fields with a prescribed algebraic relation, as counterexamples to Schanuel's conjecture over a finite-dimensional Q-space. The barrier is against soft categoricity arguments, not against the classical transcendence theorems: no conjugation or modulus appears, and Baker and six exponentials are not claimed for these models. Unlike Theorem D, the model does have an exponential function.

## Notes

**Verdict.** The specific statement was not found: no printed source has L_u, a space containing L plus a circle point, or any barrier to Diaz's conjecture. The wider clause, "a metatheorem of this type", is in print and should be cited. So "not found" holds for the theorem, but the chapter 14 entry should not imply the barrier idea is new. All leads are analogues; I claim no "implies".

**The tradition (about 1989 to 2001).**
- **Roy 1989, Propositions 1 and 2.** Seen only through DALAG and Fischler.
- **Roy 1995, section 3.2 and Theorem 3.4.** Points on xy=z^2 cannot be excluded, because they formally satisfy the rank theorem, so the method cannot prove four exponentials.
- **DALAG section 12.1.6.** Works with an abstract triple (k,K,E). Lemma 12.15 uses E in k[x] with x transcendental, which is the same device as Theorem D: a transcendental element shows a formal property is too weak. Lemma 12.16 covers dimension at most 3. Section 12.4.1 says Property (A B; C 0) would not suffice for two algebraically independent logarithms.
- **Fischler 2001, p. 186.** Says it is impossible to derive his conjectures algebraically from the rank theorem.

**Where my own translation enters (not printed).**
1. Take k=Qbar, c^2=u conj(u), x=c/u. DALAG's example E=<u,ux,ux^2> becomes H0 exactly. So Exercise 12.10 gives rank at least (d+l)/4 on H0, and hence no 2x3 configuration in H0. This is the Roy clause restricted to H0, without L~.
2. Take k=Q, x=u, p1=u in Exercise 12.9. Then E in Q[u] contains u. By Lemma 12.15 it has (A B; C 0), so no rank-one 2x2 or 2x3 matrix has independent rows and columns. I checked by hand that Baker, Hermite-Lindemann and Gel'fond-Schneider hold there: elements have positive degree, and u is transcendental over Qbar. This is a stronger property list than Theorem D's, including full four exponentials, but E misses L and is not conjugation-stable. Whether conjugation can be added is my speculation only.

**What separates the printed work from Theorem D.**
- The models do not contain L, whereas Theorem D's point is an extension of L itself.
- The formal input is a single rank or matrix property, not the bundle of named theorems.
- The conclusions are four exponentials or transcendence degree 2, not an algebraic-modulus statement.
- Conjugation never appears.

Nobody I read connects these barriers to modulus questions. Diaz 2007 states (M) and C(|u|) with no barrier remark. Diaz 2004 remarks only on how little analysis his proofs use.

**Model theory.** Bays-Kirby section 9.2 is a real barrier against soft categoricity proofs of Schanuel. It keeps an exponential function, unlike Theorem D, but does not claim the classical theorems for its models. Whether its base-field variant could encode a relation like u v = rho is my question, not their claim.

**Not a barrier.**
- Roy 1992, Theorem 6 is sharpness using actual logarithms.
- Roy 2002 limits one auxiliary-function construction, not membership statements.
- Waldschmidt 2005 only says something "does not seem" to follow.

**What I could not settle.**
- Roy 1989 is unread. It is the one source that could close the gap further, and worth obtaining on paper.
- The scholarly APIs failed (see inaccessible), so post-2007 citations of Diaz rest on web search, which found nothing on C(|u|) or (M). This coverage is weak.
- Formulas come from text layers. In Roy 1995 the OCR does not distinguish Q from Qbar, so my k=Qbar use relies on DALAG's general-k statement of Lemma 12.16 and Exercise 12.10, not on Roy's. DALAG's Exercise 12.9 hint is printed with Q[x], matching my k=Q use.
- Local files were read as text, not checked against page images. Page numbers come from running heads.
