# Entry 1, verification: V. Martello, On the rati

- **Source read:** V. Martello, On the rational Enriques-Fano threefolds discovered by Fano, arXiv:2107.04107v1 (8 Jul 2021), Lemma 6.4, PDF p. 20. Opened https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.04107. arXiv lists no journal reference; the OpenAlex check failed (HTTP 429).
- **Accessible:** True
- **Statement present:** True
- **Relation:** analogue only
- **Covers the part not found:** False
- **Passage:** If C is a twisted cubic contained in Q, then v ∈ C.

**Hypotheses.** Field: source works over C, target over Q̄ inside C; harmless, since the cubic and cone are defined over Q̄. Dimension: P³ matches a 4-dimensional V, but only via my translation. Conjugation: absent from the source; ρ = uū appears only in my translation. Genericity: the lemma has no generality assumption; the target's h is transcendental because Q̄ is algebraically closed. The source has no spaces of numbers, 2 × 3 configurations, u, h, or H₀.

**Explanation.** Lemma 6.4 is there, as a short auxiliary fact in a threefold classification: project from the vertex and a cubic cannot lie in a conic. It says nothing about the target's setting.

The link is my derivation, not the source's. Write 1, u, ρ/u as b·p_i(h) with p_i of degree ≤ 3. Then p₁p₂ = ρp₀², so the twisted cubic lies on a quadric cone. The lemma puts the vertex on the cubic, so the p_i share a root. That is one part of the degree count. The drop to quadratics, the Möbius conclusion and the partial fractions giving u², ū², 1/(u−a) are not in the source. The lemma does not state the converse classification or the Möbius step.

Not a find for the part not found. However, the translation suggests the degree count is close to classical projective geometry, which may bear on the "not routine" label. A referee should weigh that. I did not check this derivation beyond working it by hand.
