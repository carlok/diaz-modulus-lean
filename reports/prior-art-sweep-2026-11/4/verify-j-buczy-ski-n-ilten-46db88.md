**Hypotheses.** Field: K algebraically closed, so Q̄ is allowed. Dimension: ℓ=2, d=6, n=4 meets 2ℓ<d and n>2, as cited. But the theorem fixes L(D) of degree 6; the target does not. (D1) has unbounded degree: W=⟨u⁻¹,1,u,u⁷,u⁸⟩ has d=9, ℓ=5, outside ℓ≤3. NF does not even assume W is rational. Conjugation: absent, so it says nothing on Theorem F's hull or the 4-dimensional clause. Genericity: all L missing X, no genericity. The condition 1,u,u⁻¹ ∈ W is not treated. No 2×3 products, scrolls or pencils appear anywhere in the paper.

**Explanation.** The location is correct, but the theorem sorts singularities and never mentions configurations. So no implication "configuration ⇒ type" is printed, and I found no derivation of one from it.

My partial dictionary, for sextics with L=W^⊥:
- D2a: W=P'Q'·Sym²+Q'²S₁+P'²S₂, so L meets the secants on the roots of P' and of Q': two δ=1 points.
- D2b: tacnode (λ'(1,1)=1, λ'(2,2)=2), or rhamphoid cusp if P' has a double root (λ'(2)=λ'(3)=1, λ'(4)=2).
- Triple point, cusp with smooth branch, (3,4,5)-cusp: sampled L gave dim T=3, shape D1.
Exact rational computation on samples only.

Singularity type does not decide a configuration. For base-point-free T with W=⟨x,y⟩T of dimension 5, the Segre factor makes the map injective, hence no node; a random L has no D1 triple T and no pencil turned up in a finite scan.

Missing from the paper: the bound deg D≤6, δ=2 from a degree-2 ruling, and the matching above. The hard step lies in these.
